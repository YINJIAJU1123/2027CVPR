\begin{abstract}
Dexterous in-hand manipulation is an important skill for robots to reposition and use objects. However, a target specified by language may initially be hidden or blocked by the fingers, requiring the robot to explore before presenting it. In this work, we present AIMI, an agentic in-hand manipulation framework for language-guided visual exploration. The agent chooses inspection actions, identifies the target from new images, and selects a motor goal. Local controllers adjust the motor policy's commands during reasoning and execution. We evaluate our framework in simulation on six objects, each with its own motor policy. Under changed target-to-skill mappings, it achieves \QOneChangedSuccess\% mean success, exceeding the strongest evaluated baseline by \QOneChangedGain{} percentage points. We demonstrate sim-to-real transfer on four real-world objects using RGB pose feedback, achieving \HardwareAimiSuccess\% success over 80 trials, compared with \HardwareFineTunedSuccess\% for the strongest evaluated baseline.
\end{abstract}
