The semantic instruction identifies a letter. It does not specify an arbitrary target quaternion. The benchmark scores its associated canonical quaternion at a 0.16-radian tolerance. A drop is recorded when the distance from the object's nominal grasp position is at least 0.24 metres. This is an operational benchmark termination, not a detector of every transient slip or lost contact. The object may move within that boundary without a recorded drop.

\subsection{Physics and perception}
The runtime uses Isaac Lab 3.0, Isaac Sim 6.2, Newton 1.6.1, Warp 1.17, and PyTorch 2.11 with CUDA 13.0. The validated collision model contains 23 bodies and 117 shapes, comprising 116 convex shapes and one box. The contact allocation uses \texttt{nconmax=4096} and \texttt{njmax=1024}. Earlier overflow runs are invalid even if their reported rewards appear plausible.

The pose estimator is frozen. It predicts 27 image-coordinate/depth outputs for nine points, then recovers pose through calibrated geometry. The object image is rendered at $120\times120$ with live hand occlusion. Sensor calibration, object asset, policy normalization, joint order, action mapping, and smoothing belong to the release contract. A change to one of these produces a new release. The current setup does not establish real-camera robustness or unseen-scene generalization.

\subsection{Timing, termination, and exclusions}
The semantic horizon is 120 seconds of logical control time. Model latency is measured independently and translated into a minimum pending duration of $\lceil30L\rceil$ steps. The request must also have actually completed. Physics is never intentionally paused to await a semantic response. Because simulation throughput is below the logical 30~Hz rate, wall time per episode exceeds logical duration. A hardware claim requires a separately measured end-to-end rate.

The shared gate runs at 3~Hz. It requires an accepted requested-symbol binding, a non-inspection command, no outstanding request, and estimated canonical orientation error no greater than 0.16 radians. The independent scorer applies the same angular tolerance to true orientation and checks the actual requested face. No post-stop dwell is required. Success is not the minimum orientation error at any earlier time.

A service error or model call exceeding 120 seconds is recorded without replacement. The original three-system panel removes trial 183102011 from every arm. The four-way control panel removes 183102000, 183102010, and 183102011. The resulting denominators are 39 and 37. Their percentages are not directly interchangeable. Raw 40-trial outcomes remain visible, including service-affected cases.

\section{Models and Training}
\subsection{Semantic and motor inputs}
The motor policy receives 109 target-free proprioceptive/history features, seven measured pose values, and a 768-dimensional goal. Its goal projection has width 64 and a tanh nonlinearity. The inherited recurrent policy emits 21 normalized joint targets. The asset-specific goal quaternion is absent from the migrated student's inference inputs.

The hosted model route recorded in the manifests is \texttt{gpt-6-astra}. The local grounding backbone is Qwen3-VL-4B-Instruct. The Astra pathway uses public tool actions and a deterministic goal-bank lookup. The fine-tuned pathway predicts a continuous vector within the normalized skill span. Neither pathway receives the requested face's true pose. The local unreadable-view schedule is $(1,3,0,2,4,5)$ with 90-step inspections. Astra chooses inspection actions of 10--90 steps. This scheduler difference is part of the complete-system comparison and a confound for attributing all effects to model weights.

\subsection{Grounding supervision}
The grounding model first uses 4,096 teacher-view training examples and 1,024 validation examples, then 4,800 student-view training examples and 2,400 validation examples with mixed replay. Each adaptation stage uses 240 updates. Training and validation collection seeds are disjoint from the formal evaluation seeds. Readability is a geometric/image-signal proxy. The objective combines readability prediction, readable-example skill classification, and cosine alignment to a canonical vector. We do not claim human-verified text recognition labels or an action-consistency objective in this release.

The fixed layout permits learned letter-to-color shortcuts. A held-out pose or trajectory alone does not remove that possibility. A convincing grounding test changes or hides the visual evidence while holding the instruction and physical state fixed, then checks whether the model's selected target changes appropriately.

\subsection{Controller features and losses}
The sensor branch uses the current proprioceptive vector, measured pose, student belief and proposal, request-anchor pose and action, fitting residual, binding flag, pending flag, and elapsed pending duration. Its normalized inputs are clipped to $[-8,8]$. Goal features are centered and scaled using the fixed bank. The sensor, goal, and fusion widths are 192, 64, and 128. Each actor has 22 outputs: a speed logit and 21 residual values.

The waiting actor interpolates from current joint positions toward the proposal and adds a bounded residual. The execution actor applies a bounded residual to the proposal at full speed. Both are supervised from the teacher with residual regularization of 0.01. Waiting labels hold the request anchor. Execution labels follow the active goal. Paired state/goal examples and schedule-derived states are used during fitting. Their causal utility remains to be established by matched training ablations.

No raw RGB, true object pose, teacher action, future model response, or oracle semantic label is a deployed actor input. Vision affects the actor through estimated pose and recurrent belief. The reported stopping gate is measured-pose based. Previously trained completion-evidence heads are inactive and cannot be credited for the current results.

\section{Successor Student: A Separate Development Study}
The existing cube results use a fixed student checksum. Successor training is designed to ask whether extra DAgger with the actual frozen CNN sensor improves physical control beyond an equal budget with simulated pose noise. Both branches start from that same latent student, use training seed 183201, and run 400 updates with 16 environments, 45 rollout steps, and four fitting epochs. Learning rate is $3\times10^{-4}$ with a new Adam state and zero weight decay. The teacher action fraction decays as $0.9\cdot0.95^i$. Each branch collects 288,000 transitions. The CNN and teacher remain frozen.

After training, the final checkpoint from each branch is compared with the released student and the frozen latent student. Every arm uses CNN observations and 40 fresh diagnostic trials from seeds 183221 and 183222. No intermediate checkpoint is selected from those trials. The CNN branch must exceed both latent references in reach count without increasing drops to pass the development admission rule. This gate is an engineering selection rule, not a significance claim.

A selected new student still requires separately collected controller data and compatibility validation. Only then may a new semantic release use reserved seeds 183301 and 183302. The original cube-v1 results remain unchanged. Both branches and all 160 diagnostic trials have completed with valid physics logs. The four reach counts are 39/40, 38/40, 39/40, and 40/40 for the released, frozen latent, extra simulated, and extra CNN policies. Drops are one, two, one, and zero. The CNN candidate passes the stated gate and has not been automatically deployed. Exact paired tests against the frozen latent and extra simulated policies give $p=0.5$ and $p=1.0$. Thus this is a promising development result, not an established perception-matching effect.

\subsection{Why speed and joint correction are distinct}
A scalar coefficient scales the commanded joint displacement toward the student proposal. It cannot independently change the command direction. Actual motion also depends on the joint servo and contact dynamics. To quantify that restriction, we use 17,755 pending states from 61 validation trajectory groups collected with seed 185302. These states are correlated and contain no formal evaluation trials. For normalized current position $q$, student proposal $u$, and the teacher holding action $a^*$, the best scalar action in hindsight is
\begin{equation}
 \alpha^*=\clip\!\left(\frac{(a^*-q)^\top(u-q)}{\|u-q\|_2^2},0,1\right).
\end{equation}
If $u=q$, the interpolation action is $q$ for every coefficient. The resulting error is a lower bound for any scalar-speed predictor on that state. It uses privileged teacher labels for analysis and is not a deployable policy.

\subsection{Timing verification}
The latest native suite contains 344 actual calls, of which 342 are applied before episode termination. The remaining two replies belong to terminated rule-baseline episodes and are retained. The timing audit checks 52,475 control steps. Separate audits cover 80 waiting trials, 80 feedback trials and 360 visual trials. \section{Experimental Requirements Before Submission}
\subsection{Separate model strength from control}
The primary comparison uses full Astra against bare VLM baselines. Its interpretation is system-level. To attribute gains to the upper-level model, compare all providers with identical control actors, timing conventions, stopping, and information access. Preserve the existing shared-control results. Repeated live calls can produce different public plans even on matching initial states. Fixed-public-plan replay can isolate motor changes, but replay success must not be reported as a new live Astra success rate.

\subsection{Test semantic and visual causality}
The completed goal-input and restored-state tests do not establish a useful explicit controller-goal pathway. The completed visual intervention supports sensitivity to visible semantics, with only one reconstructed-image permutation. Further claims require additional mappings or objects, and an optional text-only comparison. These extensions must use new declared trials rather than select models on the completed test set.

\subsection{Test agent feedback}
The completed initial-plan comparison supports the full feedback protocol. An equal-call-budget or history/verification ablation would be needed to isolate those components. Keep the registered one-call and at-most-six-call budgets explicit and record actual calls, logical duration, wall time, and service failures. This contrasts complete protocols and does not isolate fresh information from extra inference compute. Latency replay should use the same public plan and delay sequence across motor variants. A simple time-aware residual actor provides a stronger learning baseline than fixed slowing alone. It should not be labeled an official reproduction of A2C2 or ARLI without a verified implementation.

\subsection{Separate perception accuracy and control benefit}
The independent pose study below establishes frame-level accuracy differences between two supplied systems. To test downstream benefit, compare predictors with the same student and agent. Report false stops, drops and semantic success alongside independent pose error. Oracle pose estimates the residual motor gap. Only a second experiment should adapt the student to the new predictor. A lower pose error is useful evidence, but it does not guarantee a higher task success rate.

\subsection{Objects and physical hand}
Select about five objects with variation in geometry, inertia, contact affordances, and semantic ambiguity. Record object-specific students and training budgets. Balance the task set within each object and report each row before computing an object macro-average. Forty trials per arm per object is the default batch size. Hardware runs need calibrated independent scoring, documented resets, two sessions, and synchronized video and command logs. A new object checkpoint alone is insufficient without its assets, calibration, normalization, action conventions, and goal definitions.

\section{Independent Pose Estimation under Hand Occlusion}
\label{sec:pose-occlusion}
This independent study evaluates fixed RGB and RGB-D estimators on a 58.5~mm lettered cube. Its RGB checkpoint differs from the frozen agent CNN, and its pose recalls do not update agent success counts.

\paragraph{Sampling and scoring.}
Three test seeds (31001--31003), four camera pitches ($0^\circ$, $60^\circ$, $90^\circ$, $105^\circ$), and 512 frames per group yield 6,144 observations in 48 environment clusters. RGB, RGB-D, their no-hand controls and perturbed-depth RGB-D produce 30,720 predictions. Collection records four geometry rejections; 6,139 admitted observations contain the full object.

ADD uses 6,144 nominal-cube surface points and threshold 0.1 diameter, not BOP average recall. Frozen prose used non-strict inequalities, but all evaluations consistently used strict thresholds. No samples lie on the primary boundaries.

\paragraph{Paired controls and sensitivity.}

\paragraph{Training and interpretation.}
The RGB system uses development-selected letterboxing and its existing PnP decoder. RGB-D uses the supplied epoch-1812 weights. Training histories and camera adaptation differ, so this is not a matched-budget architecture or depth-input ablation. RGB-D training manifests record 20,000 samples from seeds 43--50, validation uses seed 2027, and the test seeds do not overlap. Only nine training entries have more than 50\% occlusion. This post-hoc manifest observation is consistent with the severe-occlusion failure but is not causal evidence, and the full training imagery has not been re-audited. The old RGB training-data lineage is incomplete.

The evaluation uses simulator pose labels, ideal target segmentation, and virtual co-located depth. Real-camera calibration and Gaussian-splat appearance assets are part of the setup, so it should not be described as using no real-world information. The evidence supports improved simulated pose accuracy over this RGB deployment, with a clear severe-occlusion limit. It does not yet establish improved agent completion or real-camera accuracy. The earlier 39/40 original pose-goal versus 35/40 latent-student physical-reach diagnostic used the frozen agent CNN and is a separate experiment.

\paragraph{Evidence provenance.}
The supplied integrity report records 129 unchanged frozen files, shared finite ground truth, and pose-error recomputation within $2.28\times10^{-13}$~mm and $1.01\times10^{-11}$ degrees. Paper integration verifies file hashes and re-sums twelve shard counts. Bootstrap intervals and per-frame checks retain delivery provenance. Protocol/model hashes are archived; the final reproduction package still needs sanitized per-frame records.

\section{Native-Layout Dice Extension}
\label{sec:dice-extension}
The die is a wooden object with one through six native textured pips, rendered at $512\times512$ with the hand. Three methods each run 40 matched trials over seeds 186301 and 186302. The independent audit verifies all 120 records, canonical targets, paired reset states, source and weight hashes, response latency, and bare-baseline isolation. No trial is service-excluded.

\paragraph{Object-specific motor interface.}
The released feedforward actor uses ten frames of normalized input. Its goal-related channels are replaced by a learned reconstruction of eight conditioning features. For each frame, a 768-dimensional latent is normalized and encoded to 32 dimensions. The adapter combines it with the four measured quaternion components and their outer product, then applies layers of widths 256, 128, and 8. The original actor and observation normalization remain frozen. Inference receives physical history of shape $10\times116$ and continuous latent history of shape $10\times768$. It receives no true target quaternion, target index or table-based reconstruction of its conditioning features.

\paragraph{Perception, control and stop policy.}
The die uses the released simulator-pose noise, bias, failure, jitter and delay model. There is no image pose estimator in these trials. All upper methods receive the same RGB and image-only crop. The physical horizon is 120 seconds at 20~Hz and the stop gate is checked at 3~Hz. A reply cannot be applied before $\lceil20L\rceil$ steps, while control continues. The drop boundary and independent full-quaternion success tolerance remain 0.24 metres and 0.16 radians.

Astra uses one object-specific maintenance actor during pending and ordinary execution. Its calibrated completion model learns a bounded correction to measured angular error and an uncertainty term. It accepts when the calibrated upper bound is at most 0.16 radians. Once a correct count is declared and the state is not inspection, this gate may stop even with a further request pending. The bare baselines use no learned maintenance or completion module and stop on the measured-angle criterion only when no request is pending. Thus these are complete-system comparisons, not isolated tests of the semantic models or a replication of the cube's shared measured gate.
