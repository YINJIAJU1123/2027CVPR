\begin{abstract}
Finding and displaying a language-specified surface on a grasped object requires
the hand to reveal information while maintaining control of the object. The
target may initially face away from the camera, and a correct orientation may
still leave its identifying mark obscured by fingers. This research scaffold
defines a task in which a dexterous hand must search for the requested surface,
make it clearly visible, and sustain a stable presentation. We ask whether this
behavior can be learned without human task-action demonstrations, and whether
additional interaction improves exploration and recovery beyond the coverage
of a fixed demonstration set. A candidate approach combines language grounding,
temporally accumulated visual evidence, and reinforcement learning. Planned
comparisons include semantic scanning, a recurrent visual-language PPO policy,
and language-conditioned imitation learning. The evaluation separates target
discovery, sustained presentation, and recovery under controlled perturbations.
The method is not finalized, and no results on this task are reported here.
\end{abstract}
