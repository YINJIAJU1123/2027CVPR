Revised argument
1. In-hand manipulation can reveal object surfaces that are initially out of view or covered by fingers.
2. A specified pose or goal image does not settle how to acquire evidence for an unseen language-specified target. Continuous hand control must also work while semantic reasoning is pending.
3. ALIVE uses manipulation to gather evidence, binds the observed target to a motor goal, and then guides presentation.
4. The ViserDex teacher-student foundation and its Revo3 adaptation are explicitly attributed. The added goal-bank interface and local controllers are described separately.

Three contributions
- Agentic language-guided in-hand visual exploration that connects inspection, target binding, and presentation through successive observations.
- A goal-conditioned interface and teacher-supervised local control that combines speed adjustment with joint correction during reasoning.
- Evaluation of six object-specific simulation systems, controlled evidence for fresh images and joint correction, English/Chinese instructions on three objects, and author-confirmed demonstrations on four real objects.

Claim boundaries
The introduction does not claim that teacher-student distillation is new, that one motor policy handles all objects, or that adaptive inspection is conclusively better than fixed inspection. It does not add sustained-visibility metrics, hardware success rates, or any new experiment results. Detailed hardware reporting remains pending in Q4.
