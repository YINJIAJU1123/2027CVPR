\begin{abstract}
A language-specified target may be hidden from the camera during in-hand manipulation, requiring the robot to acquire visual evidence before presenting it. We introduce AIMI, an agentic framework that coordinates visual exploration with continuous hand control. The agent selects inspection directions and durations, uses new RGB observations and state feedback to bind the requested target to a motor goal, and guides its presentation. Teacher-supervised local controllers adjust motor commands during reasoning and execution. Controlled studies separately examine fresh visual evidence, inspection choices, and control during reasoning. Across six object-specific simulation systems, our framework achieves \QOneChangedSuccess\% mean success under changed target-to-skill mappings, exceeding the strongest evaluated baseline by \QOneChangedGain{} percentage points. We demonstrate sim-to-real transfer on four real-world objects using RGB pose feedback, achieving \HardwareAimiSuccess\% success over 80 trials, compared with \HardwareFineTunedSuccess\% for the strongest evaluated baseline.
\end{abstract}
