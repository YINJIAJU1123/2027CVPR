REVEAL — 2026-11-06 manuscript review and submission priorities

Assessment
The simulation evidence and manuscript structure are substantial, but this is still a working draft, not a submission-ready paper. The strongest current claim is online acquisition of visual evidence for semantic binding during in-hand control. A general advantage of learned waiting control, shared-policy transfer across objects, and physical-camera deployment are not established. No acceptance probability is assigned.

Evidence already available
- Six object-specific systems, original/changed semantic mappings. Perception and stopping differences are explicit.
- 2,160 language/wording trials across cube, duck and hedgehog. Full Astra exceeds fine-tuning in all eight duck/hedgehog cells after Holm correction; all four cube contrasts remain unresolved. Language non-significance is not equivalence.
- Fresh-versus-first-frame cube intervention: 32/40 versus 9/40, adjusted p = 4.7684e-7, with a common six-call cap. Actual compute and trajectories differ.
- Structural waiting ablation and independent confirmation are both retained. The latter gives full 30/39 versus fixed slowing 31/39; these outcomes cannot support a consistent controller advantage.
- Independent RGB/RGB-D estimator comparison is supporting perception evidence only.

Priority 1: close the experimental claim, not just add trials
1. Reconcile the collaborator's 80 trials per arm against per-trial source records, seed IDs, frozen checkpoints, service eligibility and protocol. Preserve the separate negative confirmation; do not pool incompatible releases or keep sampling until significance.
2. A same-model, same-controller, same-information/call-cap comparison against a simple reactive visual loop or fixed inspection schedule would isolate the agent's adaptive inspection/history contribution. Current fresh-image evidence isolates access to later images, not every agent component. This is a scientific recommendation, not a mandatory conference requirement or a newly authorized experiment.
3. Complete matched physical-hand evaluation on the actual available objects, with independent success verification, resets, calibration, drops/false stops, measured latency and synchronized videos. Keep simulation and physical outcomes separate. Camera-based pose performance is especially material because non-cube simulation uses noisy simulator pose.
4. If revised controller results remain inconclusive, retain the finding and narrow the contribution. A successful paper does not require every component to improve a metric, but its principal novelty needs direct evidence.

Priority 2: positioning and reproducibility
- Position the contribution around online observation, target binding and control. GoalDex already links language to image-goal reorientation and includes multi-object physical deployment; asynchronous execution alone is also established prior work. The current related-work discussion acknowledges this. A direct comparison should use a compatible task/interface and should not imply that baseline adaptation has already been run.
- Complete sanitized system prompts/tool schemas, sampling/runtime settings, any provider-exposed serving version, per-release checkpoint/source hashes, code instructions and asset-license inventory. Task-level language prompts are already archived; they do not replace full system schemas.
- Prepare self-contained anonymous artifacts; the colleague GitHub branch is not automatically an anonymous submission package. Do not add identity-revealing links to the submitted PDF.
- The existing staged teaser is explicitly illustrative. Quantitative/qualitative evidence is linked to recorded trials. At the author's request, the original method diagrams and supplementary planned hardware panels are restored; their captions explicitly identify unfilled physical-image slots and pending outcomes.

Current abstract and main-result summary
The abstract now emphasizes the problem and method, placing the evaluation scope and headline result in its final two sentences. It retains only 65.8% object-averaged success under changed mappings and a 40.4-percentage-point gain over the strongest evaluated baseline. The main table also reports 70.4% on original mappings. These are post-hoc descriptive equal-object means, generated from frozen counts; no SOTA or new aggregate-significance claim is made.
