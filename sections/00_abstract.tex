\begin{abstract}
A visual agent can decide which face of an object a user wants to see, but a dexterous hand must maintain contact while that decision is being computed. We study semantic in-hand reorientation with asynchronous visual reasoning and continuous physical execution. \method combines a frozen RGB pose estimator, a recurrent student conditioned on a semantic goal representation, and lightweight controllers learned for pending requests and subsequent execution. The controllers use current measurements, the active goal, and request history to modulate the student's joint targets. Privileged teacher actions supervise training without entering the deployed policy. A frozen protocol evaluates the requested semantic binding and true orientation at autonomous stopping, separately from physical reach. On one marked cube with 40 scheduled trials per system, common service-eligible success rates are 74.4\% for the full Astra system, 66.7\% for a fine-tuned VLM without the added controllers, and 33.3\% for a rule-based VLM. Learned control raises the four-arm matched estimate from 54.1\% to 73.0\%, although the paired difference is not statistically resolved. The small difference from the fine-tuned VLM is not statistically resolved.
