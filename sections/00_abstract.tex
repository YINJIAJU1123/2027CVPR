\begin{abstract}
A visual agent can identify the object face requested by a user, but a dexterous hand must maintain contact while that decision is being computed. We study semantic in-hand reorientation with asynchronous visual reasoning and continuous physical execution. \method connects a visual agent to a pose-conditioned recurrent student through a common semantic goal representation. Lightweight local controllers adapt joint targets during pending requests and subsequent execution, using measured state and public request history. Privileged teacher actions provide training supervision without entering deployment. On a marked cube with 40 matched trials per system, the latest full Astra system achieves 87.5\% autonomous semantic success, compared with 75.0\% for a bare fine-tuned VLM and 42.5\% for a bare rule VLM. A separate waiting-controller comparison improves observed success from 69.2\% to 84.6\% and reduces drops from ten to five. Feedback raises completion from 17.9\% with one initial plan to 76.9\% with up to six calls. Under a controlled letter--color remapping, Astra retains 32/40 successes while the fine-tuned model falls from 30/40 to 16/40. \end{abstract}
