\begin{abstract}
How can a robot find and present a requested object face when it is initially hidden or blocked by the fingers? We present REVEAL, a framework for agentic in-hand manipulation that connects visual search to continuous motor control. The agent chooses inspection actions, links the requested face to a motor goal, and directs its presentation. A recurrent motor policy follows this goal, while teacher-supervised local controllers adjust joint commands during agent inference and action execution. We evaluate six object-specific systems in simulation and test English and Chinese instructions on three objects. Under changed target-to-skill mappings, REVEAL achieves \QOneChangedSuccess\% success averaged across objects, exceeding the strongest evaluated baseline by \QOneChangedGain{} percentage points. Paired cube experiments show that fresh images enable additional correct target bindings, increasing success from 9/40 to 32/40. A separate controller ablation supports joint correction during inference.
\end{abstract}
